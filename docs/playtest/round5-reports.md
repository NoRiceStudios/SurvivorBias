## Marta (Wald, one war, 22 weeks, won)

She lost 34 aircraft. Her crews claimed 107 kills; 37 were real.

**What works now**
- Enemy raids hurt: works lost 67%, fuel 67% and airfields 55%. Repairs earned their cost.
- The Y-Service track record turns trusting it into a real bet. In W19 she ignored it at "1 of 8" and paid for it.
- The "wavering" warning fires once per crossing.
- Chutes match fates.
- The roll of the missing, the interlude and the portraits are good.

**What still holds it back**
- **Strategy still loses to close support.**
  - Crippled airfields come back too fast.
  - Works can't be crippled without heavy bombers.
  - A single enemy close-support raid moved the front by 31 points.
  - The +8 start hands out sectors.
- **The safe zones were not randomised in her war.**
- **Supplies pile up.**

**Her path to 9–10**
1. **Crippling should decide fronts. [big]**
   - It should cut enemy close support and lower the threshold.
   - Crippled sites should repair slowly.
   - Close support should give diminishing returns within a theater.
2. **An honest front. [big]**
   - Drop the +8 start.
   - Cap the swing one raid can cause.
   - Show the true threshold band with the Intelligence Section.
3. **A use for supplies. [small]** Done: stores convoys.
4. **Really randomised lethal zones. [small]** Done: more often.
5. **UI. [small]** Done: assignments above the fold, the verdict inside the card.

**Bugs, all fixed**
- An invalid armor zone wiped supplies.
- Damage returns were added instead of averaged.
- The liaison's word contradicted its figure.
- Help text for `fit`.
- Name overlaps.
- The interlude pop-up covered the map.

## Jonas (Seasoned, 20 weeks, two theaters won)

**What works now**
- The missing are worth waiting for again.
- Penrose came back through the lines in week 17, then was lost on his first flight back. It was his strongest moment of any round.
- Station life: the girl in a summer dress, the empty chair, and "the old hands have stopped learning the new ones' names".
- Portraits.

**What still holds it back**
- Commanding officers died 2.5 times as often as other crews, because near-empty squadrons sent their COs out in one of two aircraft.
- Repeated scenes and sayings; a dead man's saying passed to his successor.
- Obituaries missed the decision that mattered.
- Confidence sat at 95–100.

**His path to 9–10**
1. **Cap CO deaths. [small]** Done: the CO stays home with fewer than three aircraft, and is harder to bring down.
2. **No repeats. [small]** Done.
3. **The obituary names the decision closest to the death. [small]** Done.
4. **Attrition that costs something in the war. [big]** Partly done: losses are now judged against the wing's size, and a gutted bomber arm gets no strike orders.
5. **Station life that reacts to the dead. [big]** Partly done: wakes for COs, and the evening after a bad week.

**Bugs, all fixed**
- "0 operations"
- "badly damaged" against "working order"
- "into the drink" over land
- empty squadrons
- absurd claims
- missing-list wording
- the Tally-ho escort line
- the stand-in CO who vanished
- the morale note for an empty squadron
